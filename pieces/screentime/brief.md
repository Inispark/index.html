# The Glowing Rectangle — brief

**Spec:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24fps, ~46s, sketchbook style, voice-led morph chain (F2 grammar: 8 eras, 7 shape morphs, no hard cuts). Score composed in code under a narrated voice-over (`voice/script.json`; scratch espeak voice for timing until the ElevenLabs takes arrive).

**Idea:** how screen time works on a kid's brain — the chase, the unfinished brakes, the lost sleep — and that a young brain is built to change.
**Device:** the screen's yellow glow is the only light on the page until it lifts off the face-down tablet and becomes the morning sun.
**Colour:** yellow = the screen's glow (and the one highlighted word per caption); terracotta = Sam only.

## Sound plan
Night hum → a pluck per swipe (chime on WOW) → 8ths + bass through the brain, building → 16ths + hats at night/cost (busiest) → dead stop on "focus" → silence ~1.2s → chord stab + sub as the glow becomes the sun (loudest) → flat warm chord, ticks on sleep/play/faces → the opening hum in major, Sam's rising fifth.

## Claims + sources
- Tweens (8–12) average about five hours of screen media a day — Common Sense Media census (2019: 4h44m screen media; newer report ≈5h). https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/the-common-sense-census-media-use-by-tweens-and-teens-new-research-finds-youtube-videos-beat-out-tv-and · https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/screen-time-report-dives-into-how-tweens-teens-are-using-ai-social-media-and-more-common-sense-media-census-tech-and-media-use-by-tweens-and-teens
- Dopamine responds to uncertain / anticipated reward (reward prediction error; variable reward schedules) — Schultz; Skinner. Summary: https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/variable-rewards . Phrased as mechanism, not as "apps are addictive".
- Prefrontal cortex keeps developing into the mid-20s, gradually, varying by person — https://www.simplypsychology.org/?p=86346
- Evening light suppresses melatonin; children appear more sensitive (≈2× adults in one study) — https://sleepfoundation.org/articles/how-blue-light-affects-kids-sleep . Counterpoint: 15 min tablet use didn't suppress toddlers' melatonin — https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/brief-evening-screen-time-did-not-suppress-melatonin-in-toddlers . Hence "extra sensitive", not "screens wreck sleep".
